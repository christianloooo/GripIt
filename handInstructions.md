Grip explanation
Four-seam fastball
Ball orientation: Find a horseshoe-shaped section of stitching by tracing its U-shaped curve. Rotate the ball so the horseshoe lies sideways across your fingers, with its rounded end toward your thumb and its opening toward your pinky. Index finger: Curve it over the ball and place its fingerprint pad across the raised stitching, so the seam crosses underneath the pad rather than running along the finger. Middle finger: Place it beside the index finger, also with its pad crossing the stitching. Let its longer fingertip extend naturally farther around the ball. Keep a comfortable, small separation between the fingers. Thumb: Place its fleshy pad underneath the ball, approximately opposite the space between the two upper fingers, initially on smooth leather. Ring finger: Curl it against the pinky-side surface, using its side for light support. Pinky: Relax it beside the ring finger, clear of the ball. Depth and pressure: Hold the ball primarily between the upper finger pads and thumb without deliberately burying it in your palm. Apply enough pressure to prevent slipping without making your hand rigid. Tactile check: Both upper pads feel stitching crossing beneath them; the thumb supports underneath; the ring finger supports the side. 
Two-seam fastball
Ball orientation: Find the narrow area where two stretches of stitching run relatively close together. Rotate the ball until those stretches run approximately from your wrist toward your fingertips. Index finger: Place its pad along the thumb-side seam of that pair, following the stitching’s direction. Middle finger: Place its pad along the other seam, toward the pinky side. Unlike the four-seamer, the seams run approximately along the fingers rather than across them. Slightly rotate the ball until both pads engage stitching comfortably; do not force your fingers into an uncomfortable spread. Thumb: Support the underside with its fleshy pad, approximately opposite the two upper fingers. Start on smooth leather and adjust slightly for balance. Ring finger: Curl it lightly against the pinky-side surface. Pinky: Keep it relaxed beside the ring finger, off the ball. Depth and pressure: Begin at a similar depth to the four-seamer, with comfortable pressure from both upper fingers against the thumb. Tactile check: Trace along each upper finger with your other hand: each should approximately follow its own seam. Finger pads directly on the seams and pads against their inside edges are both common variations. 
Slider
Ball orientation: For this common version, begin with the narrow seam area used for a two-seamer. Locate the seam on the pinky side of that area. Middle finger: Place its pad along that seam, obtaining a definite purchase on the stitching. Index finger: Bring it close beside the middle finger, toward the thumb side, with its pad resting on the adjacent smooth leather. The fingers may touch lightly. Both fingers are grouped toward one seam rather than occupying separate seams as in the two-seamer. Thumb: Place it underneath and opposite the upper fingers, slightly off-center if that feels comfortable. It should support the ball without requiring a hard pinch. Ring finger: Curl it against the pinky-side surface below the middle finger, using its side as support. Pinky: Relax it beside the ring finger, clear of the ball. Depth and pressure: Maintain secure middle-finger contact against the seam, opposed by the thumb, while the index finger stabilizes. Choose a comfortable depth; there is no compulsory palm gap. Tactile check: Middle pad on stitching, index pad on leather immediately beside it, fingers close together, thumb underneath. This specifies a grip; the release also determines whether the resulting pitch behaves like a slider. 
Changeup — circle-change version
Ball orientation: Find the narrow area with two nearby stretches of stitching and orient them approximately along your fingers. Middle finger: Place its pad along the thumb-side seam of the pair. Ring finger: Place its pad along the pinky-side seam. These become the principal upper contacts, replacing the index–middle pair used for the two-seamer. Let the shorter ring finger contact the ball naturally without forcing its tip level with the middle fingertip. Index finger: Curl it around the thumb-side surface rather than placing it over the top. Thumb: Wrap it around the lower thumb-side surface, supporting the ball with its fleshy surface. Bring the curled index fingertip into comfortable contact with the thumb to form a small loop beside the ball. Do not force a perfectly round circle. Pinky: Wrap it lightly around the opposite side to stabilize the ball. Depth and pressure: Let the ball settle comfortably into the hand while maintaining middle- and ring-finger contact. Hold securely without squeezing the index–thumb loop into a rigid clamp. Tactile check: Middle and ring fingers over the ball, index–thumb loop on the thumb side, pinky supporting the opposite side. Changeups have many other valid grip variations.
