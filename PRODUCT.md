# GripIt: Product Guide

## What it is

GripIt helps baseball players find a better grip.

You scan your hand. You see a 3D hand holding a baseball, fitted to your hand size. You throw. You type in the numbers from your Rapsodo. GripIt shows you which parts of your grip to work on next.

The goal: better spin, more consistent pitches, and better Rapsodo numbers.

## Who it is for

- Pitchers who train with a Rapsodo and want to know what to change.
- Coaches who want to show a player exactly where the fingers go.
- Players with smaller or larger hands who find standard grip diagrams don't fit.

## The problem

Grip diagrams show one hand. Your hand is different. Rapsodo tells you the result of a pitch, but not what to change in your hand. Players guess.

GripIt connects the two: what your hand looks like, and what the pitch did.

## The flow, step by step

### Step 1. Scan your hand

1. Open the app and tap "Scan my hand".
2. Hold your open hand next to a baseball. The ball sets the size.
3. The app measures your fingers and palm.
4. If you don't have a ball handy, tap "Use sample hand" and keep going.

What you see: your hand measurements, shown on screen. Nothing to type. Your video stays on your device.

### Step 2. Pick a pitch and see your grip

1. Choose a pitch (the demo starts with the four-seam fastball).
2. The app shows a 3D hand holding a baseball, fitted to your hand.
3. Drag to spin it around. Zoom in and out.
4. Tap a finger to see what it should do. For example:
   - Index: pad across the seam, medium pressure.
   - Middle: next to the index, press a bit firmer.
   - Thumb: underneath, light. Don't squeeze.
   - Palm: leave a small gap of air.

What you see: a grip made for your hand, with a short tip on every part.

### Step 3. Throw and enter your numbers

1. Pick up the ball. Use the grip. Throw.
2. Look at the Rapsodo screen.
3. Type in the numbers. One throw is enough.

The numbers we ask for:
- Velocity
- Total spin
- Spin direction
- Spin efficiency (or gyro degree)
- Release height

Optional: the score your Rapsodo app shows, so you can compare before and after.

The app checks for typos. If you type 900 mph, it asks you to check.

More throws make the result steadier. With 3 or more throws, the app also checks how consistent you are.

### Step 4. See what to work on

1. The same 3D hand comes back, now colored.
   - Green: looks fine.
   - Yellow: worth a look.
   - Red: work on this first.
2. Tap a colored finger to see why. You'll see which number triggered it, how sure we are, and what to try.
3. The app gives you one thing to change for your next throws. Just one.
4. Throw again. Enter the new numbers. See if the colors cool down.

What you see: a clear "focus area" map, and one next step.

## Example

Maya throws a four-seam fastball. Her Rapsodo shows 72% spin efficiency. Most four-seam coaching aims for 90% or more.

GripIt colors her wrist and middle finger red, and index and thumb yellow. The tip says: "Stay behind the ball. Firm wrist. Push through the back of it."

She tries it. Next throw: 84%. The colors turn yellow. She keeps going.

## What we promise, and what we don't

We say:
- "Fitted to your hand."
- "Focus areas."
- "Try this next."

We don't say:
- "The perfect grip."
- "This is what you did wrong."
- "This will raise your Rapsodo score."

Why: the numbers from a throw can't prove which finger caused a problem. Our colors are well-informed guesses based on coaching advice, and every one shows how confident we are. Some problems, like a drifting release point, come from the arm and timing, not the grip. We label those "not grip".

## The 30-second demo

1. (0:00) "This is my hand." Show the scan result or sample hand.
2. (0:08) "Here's a four-seam grip made for it." Rotate. Tap a finger.
3. (0:16) "I threw. Here are my Rapsodo numbers." Type them in.
4. (0:22) "Here's where to focus." Show the colors and the one tip.
5. (0:28) "Throw again and watch it cool down."

Keep a sample hand and a "load sample throw" button ready. Label sample data as sample data.

## Why it could be a business

- Every session records hand size, grip and results. Over time that becomes a dataset nobody else has: which grips work for which hands.
- That data could train better advice later.
- Natural partners: Rapsodo, TrackMan, coaches, training facilities.
- Other players in the space: Pitch AI, and Driveline's grip work. We differ by fitting the grip to your hand and linking it to your launch monitor results.

## What is built today, and what is next

Built:
- Hand scan with a baseball for size.
- 3D hand and baseball with fitted grips and a rotate-and-zoom view.
- The scoring logic that turns Rapsodo numbers into colored focus areas.

Next:
- Tap a finger to see its tip.
- The Rapsodo number entry screen.
- The colored results view and the one-step advice.
- Rules for more pitches (slider, changeup, curveball). Today the rules cover the fastball and curveball.
- A pitcher reviews the tips before we call them advice.
